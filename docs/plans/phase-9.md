## 8. A center pivot's percent timer (done 2026-11-22)

How pivot operators set what they apply. A center pivot's panel sets its speed as a percent: at 100% the end tower runs all the time and the pivot makes its fastest circle, putting on the least water; at 50% it runs half of each minute, so a circle takes twice as long and puts on twice the depth. Growers know their pivot by its sprinkler chart or a rule of thumb ("0.2 in at 100%", or "a circle takes 18 hours at 100%"), and think "I ran North at 40%", not in inches or hours. Depth = depth at 100% × 100 ÷ percent, for a whole circle.

- [x] **On the system:** a center pivot's (and a linear's) hours for a full circle (or pass) at 100%, optional, in Equipment. With the pump capacity WISP already has, that gives the depth at 100% through the run hours conversion (`PivotIrrigation#irrigated_acres`), so nothing else is asked; or the depth at 100% straight from the chart, if growers know that better (ask the beta which).
- [x] **In daily entry and on the system page:** a third choice beside inches and run hours, "Percent timer", with the circles or fraction run (default one), shown and stored as run hours (circle hours × 100 ÷ percent × circles) so the balance and exports need nothing new; the entry shows the inches it comes to. Check the conversion against a real chart with a grower first.

*(Built 2026-11-22 on branch `percent-timer`, for Ben's look, with Ben's choices: the depth at 100% stored on the system rather than circle hours, with "Work it out from the hours a circle takes" (pump capacity over the system's mapped area) in the form; % timer as a third unit beside inches and hours (day, week and system page), not a popup, converted when read like run hours; the field rows' own amounts in daily entry take a percent while their system's is one (sector speed control), saved as inches with the percent kept; and the system page's Applied to became per-field amounts. The info tip and glossary say it's the speed setting, not a VRI zone's nozzle rate. Still to do: check the conversion against a real chart with a grower, and ask the beta whether they know the depth or the circle time.)*

**The chance of rain.** `ForecastDays`' chance is the share of the GFS ensemble's 31 members with rain at the zone's grid point that day (the members' own rain: Q10's correction scales only their ET0). It is the model's agreement, not the National Weather Service's PoP. PoP is the chance of measurable rain at any one point in the area, so it already counts both confidence and how much of the area gets rain ("60%" can mean sure of scattered showers). The ensemble's grid is coarse (about 25 km), so a member's scattered storms come out as a little rain over the whole cell: on showery days many members are wet and the chance runs high, while any one field may stay dry. The GFS ensemble is also known to be overconfident (members agree more than the weather does). And the threshold was 0.01 in, which is nothing to an irrigator. So the chance is now **of 0.1 in or more** (`ForecastDays::WET_IN`, on every strip), which drops the drizzle days and is the amount that wets the soil surface (`SoilEvaporation`); the glossary's "Chance of rain" says what it is and isn't.
